## Post 1

Link- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_what-makes-cold-emails-resonate-address-activity-7454972452759142400-U5bE

What makes cold emails resonate?

Address the elephant in the room.

That means saying what your prospect is already thinking
but hasn’t said yet.

If there were an elephant in the room, you wouldn’t ignore it.

You’d say:

“What’s this elephant doing in the room?”

In cold email, it means naming the concern before they have to.

Why this works:

When people feel like you’re trying to persuade them,
their guard goes up.

They start scanning for what’s wrong.
Looking for the catch.
Protecting their time.

But when you say the objection first…

You remove the threat.

You show:
“I’m not here to trap you.”

And something subtle happens:

They don’t feel the need to defend.

Instead of thinking,
“What’s this person trying to sell me?”

They think,
“Okay… they get it.”

Examples:

“You might be using voicemail or an answering service after hours, and it’s been working well enough.”

“This might feel expensive compared to letting calls roll to voicemail overnight.”

“You might be hesitant to have something automated booking jobs without your team involved.”

You’re not pushing.

You’re aligning with their internal dialogue.

And when someone feels understood,
they’re far more open to understanding you.

Just because you don’t call out the elephant in the room
doesn’t mean it’s not there.

## Insight
- Preempt objections to lower resistance. Naming what prospects already think builds trust and reduces defensiveness. Alignment beats persuasion—when they feel understood, they’re more open to your message.

## Post 2

Link- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-braun_the-job-of-the-first-sentence-of-a-cold-email-activity-7454572695637159936-y9Tb

The job of the first sentence of a cold email 
is to motivate people to read the second sentence.

That’s it.

So how do you do that?

Let’s see if I can make you curious enough to keep going.

This is only for people who buy avocados.

Ready?

“You ever save half an avocado knowing you’re going to throw it away tomorrow?”

For some of you this sentence creates that head nod moment of:

“Yeah, I do that.”
“Wait, that’s me. What do you have?”

That’s the feeling you want in cold email.

The best first lines don’t explain.
They point.

To a small, specific problem 
your prospect instantly recognizes.

Because when someone sees themselves in your first sentence

They read the second one. 

## Insight
- The first line should trigger recognition, not explain. Point to a specific, relatable pain so prospects see themselves instantly—curiosity and relevance pull them into the next sentence.
