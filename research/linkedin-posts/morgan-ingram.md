## Post 1

Link- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/morganjingramamp_the-c-suite-looks-at-the-sdr-team-and-sees-activity-7450538528180146177-ThHX

The C-suite looks at the SDR team and sees a pipeline machine. However, if it's the only way you are looking at it my belief is that it is a big mistake.

Because you're missing the most valuable thing sitting right in front of you.

A talent pipeline.

Think about this... 

Every great AE, every strong sales manager, every VP of Sales who actually knows how to build... most of them started as SDRs. 

The SDR role is where you learn:

• How to handle rejection 
• Understand human nature 
• Think on your feet
• Communicate under stress
• Build something from nothing
 
All those skills are future leaders skills to me...

Ok sure yeah you want to leverage AI in the sales org. 

Great... Who exactly is going to be inputting the knowledge into the AI? Who understands the buyer well enough to train the prompts? Who knows what a great cold call sounds like well enough to build a framework around it?

You might want to develop people who actually understand the craft before handing them technology to scale it.

This is exactly why I built Sales Team Six the way I did. 45 days developing the whole person.. not just the skill set. Because the reps who understand the craft become the leaders who scale it.

Yet most companies treat the SDR team like a secondhand option. 

You're burning the talent pipeline AND wondering why your AI strategy doesn't work..

When I managed 13 SDRs that team never missed their number... But the thing I'm most proud of isn't the pipeline we built...

Reps from that room got promoted to AEs... Some are leaders running their own teams now.

If you develop SDRs as whole people... teach them how to think not just what to say... two things happen.

They produce better pipeline right now.
AND they become the leaders you won't have to hire from the outside later.
Sales development isn't just a function.

It's the farm system for your entire revenue organization.

Treat it like one.

## Insight
- SDR teams aren’t just pipeline engines—they’re leadership incubators. Developing thinking, not scripts, improves current performance and builds future sales leaders who can scale systems and effectively leverage AI.

## Post 2

Link- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/morganjingramamp_im-watching-us-create-an-entire-generation-activity-7442566288687902720-aL7I

I'm watching us create an entire generation of B2B sales reps who can write AI prompts but can't tell you why their buyer should care. And it's bothering me.

I am team AI. When used right it's awesome... Yet when I speak to most leaders they say their teams are "doing AI" but the reps have no context on why they're using it.
 
That's not a strategy. That's a science experiment with your pipeline.
 
Before you hand a rep an AI tool.. they need to do these 3 things first:
 
1. Manually get 25 responses from email, LinkedIn or cold calls.
 
You should have to earn your way to AI. If a rep doesn't know why their buyer buys.. if they don't understand how the company makes money.. AI is going to make things worse not better.
 
I'm seeing reps go two hours deep into AI prompts and produce nothing. That's not efficiency. That's a waste of everyone's time.
 
This is what we focus on first inside Sales Team Six. Before AI touches the process.. the rep needs to understand how to connect with a human.
 
2. Get creative with 10-20 personal touchpoints per month.
 
Send a video. Make a personalized GIF. Do something that makes the prospect go "ok that was different."
 
AI cannot do critical thinking. It can only be critical with the thinking YOU provide. If you have nothing to give it.. it gives you nothing back.
 
AI is a mirror.
 
3. Articulate why a prospect should care... without AI.
 
If a rep can't explain in their own words why someone should take a meeting.. an AI prompt isn't going to do it for them.
 
Once a rep can do all three? Now give them AI. Now it amplifies something real. Now you're cooking.
 
But we are doing a disservice to the sales community by forcing reps to use AI when they don't even know why they're doing their job in the first place.
 
Because the most dangerous thing happening in B2B sales right now isn't AI replacing reps.
 
It's reps using AI before they understand what they're actually selling.

## Insight
- AI amplifies understanding, not replaces it. Without firsthand selling experience, buyer insight, and clear value articulation, reps misuse AI—creating noise instead of results. Master fundamentals before scaling with tools.
