## Post 1

Link- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/williamallred_one-of-the-biggest-problems-with-your-cold-activity-7442903238540013568-A5Ux

One of the biggest problems with your cold email? The message isn't adapted to the level of the recipient. Let's say I sold Navan (travel & expense automation software) to Finance teams.

The CFO
The Director of FP&A
A Finance Ops Analyst

They're all in finance.
What Navan "does" is still the same.

But how you explain it changes massively.

The CFO? How will it give their team time back to ensure they're on track for their 2 year plan?

FP&A Director? How's this going to reduce unexpected T&E spikes and dips to make smarter forecasts? 

The Ops Analyst? How's it going to keep me from having to chase down expense details?

The strategy
The forecast
The day to day work

3 different ways of telling the same story.

The reality? 

Most sellers say "I get it"

Most sellers can't capture this nuance.

They don't know their buyer well enough.

Product marketing? Never heard of em, but if they could write how we should talk as sellers, they'd probably be able to help ;-)

PS. LLMs suck at this because they're fed by a bunch of bad materials on the web. The truth is the best insights have to be fed from either 1st party data or unique insight from within the org.

## Insight
- Messaging must match buyer context, not product. Same solution, different value per role—strategic, analytical, operational. Generic messaging fails; deep customer understanding and first-party insight drive resonance, not templated AI outputs.
